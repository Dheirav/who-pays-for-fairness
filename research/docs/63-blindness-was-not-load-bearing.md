## The confound, and the cell that removes it

The paper's regime contrast changed two things at once: both attribute-aware cells were
post-processing, both blind cells the reduction. "The boundary is attribute access" and
"the boundary is the optimizer family" were observationally equivalent. The missing
cell: the same reduction (ExpGrad, DP, ε=0.01) on document 43's eighteen populations
with the protected attribute in the feature set. Both readings' bars committed in
advance: theory |r| < 0.30; method r ≥ +0.40; dead zone declared.

## The score

**R1 — the theorem's group directions: 18/18 HOLDS.** Advantaged loses, disadvantaged
gains, in every population, at the "all of them" bar.

**R2 — r = +0.672 across the 13 retained natural arms.** The rate→pool relationship
*survives* attribute access under the same optimizer — if anything stronger than the
blind cell's +0.585. **Method reading, as pre-registered.**

## What this settles

* **The pool-direction boundary is the optimizer family, not blindness.** The −0.024
  vanishing is a property of threshold re-derivation (post-processing), not of reading
  the attribute. The paper's two-round-old framing "the rule stops where the model may
  read the attribute" is corrected in place — §Regime, Scope, and the abstract all
  restated.
* **The theory's own claim comes out stronger.** The theorem constrains each *group's*
  direction wherever the attribute is readable — and that now holds in every aware cell
  measured: 27/27 post-processing (9 pre-registered) plus 18/18 aware in-processing.
  The two results separate cleanly: groups determined, pool rate-predictable under
  in-processing in both regimes.
* **The claim's applicability widens.** One sealed cohort says the rule works for
  attribute-aware in-processing too. The paper keeps its attribute-blind scope (where
  the record lives, and where deployed systems sit) and states the widening at one
  cohort's strength.
* The 2×2 has an unfillable cell — blind post-processing does not exist (the method
  needs the attribute at decision time) — so "method family" is as far as the design
  can be pushed; within in-processing, attribute access is now tested on both sides.
