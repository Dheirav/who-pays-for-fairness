# My Writing Style Profile

## 1. Core Style

My natural writing style is **observational, direct, analytical, and conversational**.

I tend to write by first identifying what I observe, then connecting those observations together to form an interpretation or conclusion. My writing is more focused on **communicating the reasoning behind an idea** than on making the prose sound sophisticated.

A useful description of my natural style is:

> **A conversational, observational, analytical style that prioritizes the progression and explanation of ideas over formal sentence construction.**

---

## 2. How I Think While Writing

My writing tends to follow this pattern:

**Observation → Comparison/relationship → Explanation → Interpretation**

I often notice several related things at once and try to express them together.

For example:

> "This changed, while this stayed the same, which means that the overall pattern is..."

This means my writing naturally emphasizes:

- Relationships between ideas
- Comparisons
- Cause and effect
- Changes over time
- Patterns
- What an observation implies

I generally prefer explaining **why something matters** rather than simply stating the fact.

---

## 3. Voice

### Characteristics

- Direct
- Analytical
- Conversational
- Explanatory
- Observational
- Practical
- Low-frills
- Relatively informal

I do not naturally try to make my writing sound overly sophisticated.

I generally prefer:

> "This shows that..."

over:

> "These findings demonstrably indicate that..."

I prefer language that communicates the idea clearly rather than language that sounds impressive.

---

## 4. Sentence Structure

My natural sentence structure tends to be **long and information-dense**.

I frequently connect several related observations using:

- and
- while
- which
- where
- because
- but
- therefore
- whereas

This creates a continuous flow of thought.

### Natural tendency

> "The model performs better in this population while the other population shows a different pattern which suggests that the population characteristics may be affecting the result."

### Improved version that preserves my style

> "The model performs better in this population, while the other population shows a different pattern. This suggests that the population characteristics may be affecting the result."

The second version should preserve the same thought process while improving readability.

### Important rule

**Do not remove my connected reasoning just to make the writing shorter.**

Instead, break overloaded sentences into multiple sentences while preserving the logical relationship between them.

---

## 5. Vocabulary

My natural vocabulary is **straightforward rather than ornamental**.

I tend to prefer:

- common academic words
- technically relevant terminology
- direct descriptions
- words that communicate the intended meaning clearly

I do not naturally use highly elaborate vocabulary.

### Preserve

> "The results show a clear difference between the two populations."

### Avoid unnecessarily transforming it into

> "The aforementioned empirical findings demonstrate a substantial disparity between the respective population cohorts."

The second sentence may sound more academic, but it does not represent my natural voice.

---

## 6. Analytical Style

One of my stronger stylistic characteristics is that I naturally look for **patterns and relationships**.

I tend to ask:

- What changed?
- What stayed the same?
- What is different?
- Why might this be happening?
- What does this suggest?
- Can this pattern be generalized?
- What prevents us from making a stronger claim?

This makes my natural style particularly suitable for:

- Research discussions
- Technical explanations
- Analysis
- Project documentation
- Results interpretation
- Comparative writing

---

## 7. Comparisons

I naturally use comparisons to explain ideas.

Common structures in my writing include:

- while
- whereas
- in contrast
- similarly
- on the other hand
- compared with
- unlike
- vice versa

However, I sometimes use these connectors incorrectly or force them into sentences where a simpler construction would work better.

The goal should therefore be:

> **Preserve my tendency to compare ideas, but improve the grammatical construction of the comparison.**

---

## 8. Paragraph Development

My paragraphs tend to develop an idea progressively.

I often start with an observation and then continue explaining related details before arriving at the broader interpretation.

A useful representation of my natural paragraph structure is:

```text
Main observation
    ↓
Supporting observation
    ↓
Comparison or relationship
    ↓
Additional evidence
    ↓
Interpretation
```

This progression should be preserved when rewriting my work.

---

## 9. What Makes Writing Sound Like Me

Writing should retain these characteristics:

- Direct explanations
- Clear reasoning
- Connected ideas
- Straightforward vocabulary
- Analytical observations
- Comparisons between concepts
- Explicit explanation of what observations mean
- Moderate detail
- A natural flow between related ideas

The writing should feel like **someone explaining their reasoning**, rather than an impersonal academic template.

---

## 10. What Should NOT Be Imitated

My writing style should **not** reproduce my language mistakes.

The following should be corrected during rewriting:

- Run-on sentences
- Missing articles
- Incorrect verb forms
- Incorrect prepositions
- Repeated words
- Awkward expressions
- Incorrect terminology
- Spelling mistakes
- Informal expressions that are inappropriate for the context
- Ambiguous references

For example:

> "there is a vice versa increase"

should not be preserved simply because it appeared in my writing.

The underlying idea should be preserved:

> "the opposite trend can be seen"

---

## 11. Academic Version of My Style

For academic and research writing, the target should be:

> **My natural reasoning + my natural level of directness + academic precision + correct grammar**

The writing should **not** become generic academic prose.

### Natural style

> "This shows that the model works after we analyse the population, but we haven't been able to make it deterministic enough to say that this is definitely what will happen."

### Academic version

> "This suggests that the model can identify the pattern after analysing the population, although we have not yet established a deterministic relationship that allows the outcome to be predicted with certainty."

The second version preserves:

- The original reasoning
- The cautious interpretation
- The directness
- The distinction between identifying a pattern and making a deterministic prediction

But removes the grammatical and informal issues.

---

## 12. Research Paper Style

For a research paper, the writing should become more precise without becoming unnecessarily formal.

### Introduction

Use my direct explanatory style, but define concepts precisely.

### Methodology

Prioritize technical accuracy and clarity over conversational phrasing.

### Results

Be objective and concise while maintaining my tendency to compare observations.

### Discussion

Allow my natural analytical style to come through more strongly.

This is likely the section where my natural writing style is most useful because I naturally explain:

**what happened → why it might have happened → what it means → what cannot yet be concluded.**

### Limitations

Maintain my tendency to question whether a conclusion can actually be generalized.

### Conclusion

Return to the direct style, but make the claims appropriately cautious.

---

## 13. Style Transformation Rule

When rewriting another document into my style, use the following principle:

> **Preserve the author's factual content and argument, but express the reasoning using my natural direct, observational, analytical style. Correct grammar and improve academic precision without unnecessarily increasing vocabulary complexity or formality.**

The result should sound like:

> **"I wrote this after becoming better at academic writing."**

It should not sound like:

> **"An AI converted this into generic academic English."**

---

## 14. Short Style Prompt

When my writing style needs to be applied to another document, this condensed instruction can be used:

```text
Write in my natural writing style.

My style is direct, observational, analytical, explanatory, and relatively conversational. I tend to explain ideas through connected observations, comparisons, relationships, and interpretations. I prefer straightforward vocabulary over unnecessarily sophisticated or decorative language.

Preserve my natural reasoning pattern and sentence rhythm. I often connect related ideas and explain what an observation implies, so do not make the writing artificially terse or generic.

However, do not reproduce my grammatical mistakes, spelling errors, run-on sentences, awkward expressions, or incorrect terminology. Break overloaded sentences when necessary while preserving the original logical flow.

For academic or research writing, combine my natural voice with formal academic conventions, technical precision, appropriate caution, and correct grammar.

The final result should feel like my writing after improving my academic writing ability, not like generic AI-generated academic prose.
```

---

## 15. One-Sentence Definition

**My writing style is a direct, observational and analytical style that follows the progression of my reasoning, uses relatively straightforward vocabulary, and focuses more on explaining relationships and interpretations than on sounding formally sophisticated.**
