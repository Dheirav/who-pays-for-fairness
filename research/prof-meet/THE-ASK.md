# The decision I need

## The question

**Is "we found *when* this happens" enough on its own to publish, or does the paper need
"and here is *why*" first?**

## Why it is a genuine fork

**If "when" is enough**, the paper is close to ready. The claim — that these fairness fixes
take opportunities away only when the system is stingy, and hand them out when it is
generous — is backed by a clean experiment, checked on 26 datasets, and confirmed on
fourteen that were run only after the prediction was written down. What remains is writing,
diagrams, and one missing comparison.

**If "why" is needed first**, that is a different project. My attempt to explain the flip
failed: it passed the test I set for it and was then beaten by a much cruder rule. Doing it
properly would need datasets where two things that normally move together can be pulled
apart, which is a harder experiment than anything I have run.

## What I would argue

The "when" is the contribution, and it stands on its own:

1. **It corrects a well-known claim.** A 2023 Oxford paper put "by default" in its title. It
   is not a default — it is conditional, and they happened to be working inside the one
   setting where their version holds.
2. **It is a controlled experiment, not a correlation.** One thing changes and test code
   verifies that everything else is identical.
3. **It has a practical reading that needs no explanation of the mechanism.** Loans, hiring
   and admissions are nearly all stingy systems — so the setting where these fixes take
   opportunities away is the setting they are actually used in.

Plenty of published work establishes when something happens without establishing why.

## The objection I expect

That a rule without an explanation is just a curve, and that "somewhere between 0.25 and
0.60" is too vague to act on.

That is fair. My answer is that the *direction* is what changes a real decision, not the
exact tipping point — but I would rather be told now whether that holds up than write the
paper around it and find out later.

## Other questions, if there is time

1. **Which venue, and when?** The deadline decides the page limit, which decides how much of
   16 research documents survives into the paper.
2. **One missing comparison.** The closest competing paper implements its fix a different
   way, and I have never run that version directly. About an hour of work. Worth doing?
3. **AI assistance.** I used an AI assistant substantially — for writing code, running
   analyses and drafting. What does the department expect me to disclose, and how?

## What is not in question

The measurements. Every headline number is regenerated from stored results by an automated
check that fails if a document and its data disagree.

Three of my own predictions failed and are written up as failures. Two of my own claims were
withdrawn. I would rather be told the framing is wrong now than find out in review — which
is exactly what happened with the remedy, and why I no longer claim it as new.
