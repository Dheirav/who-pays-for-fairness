# The decision I need

## The question

**Is the scope condition enough on its own for FAccT or AIES, or should I establish the
mechanism first?**

## Why it is a real fork

**If the scope condition is enough**, the paper is close to submittable. The claim —
levelling down reverses above a selection rate of roughly 0.3 — is established by a
single-factor design, replicated across two protected attributes, five states, a second
domain, and fourteen held-out populations. The remaining work is writing, figures, and one
missing baseline.

**If the mechanism is needed first**, that is a different project. My derivation cleared its
pre-registered bars and was then beaten by a constant rule, so I have no explanation. Making
one would require populations where curvature and selection rate come apart — score
distributions with the same operating rate and different shapes — which is a harder
experiment than anything run so far.

## What I would argue

The scope condition is the contribution, and it stands on its own for three reasons:

1. It **corrects a widely-cited claim**. Mittelstadt et al. put "by default" in their title;
   the direction is conditional, and they were working inside the one regime where their
   framing holds.
2. It is **causally identified**, not correlational. One factor moves and the test code
   asserts the rest is fixed.
3. It has a **policy reading that does not need a mechanism**: lending, hiring and
   admissions are low-selection-rate settings, so the regime where these constraints take
   decisions away is the regime they are deployed in.

Plenty of accepted empirical work establishes *when* something happens without establishing
*why*.

## The counter-argument I expect

A reviewer may say a scope condition without a mechanism is a curve, and that the crossover
value (0.25–0.60) is too imprecise to act on. That is fair. My answer is that the direction
is what changes a deployment decision, not the third decimal place of the threshold — but I
would rather hear whether that holds up before writing the paper around it.

## Secondary questions, if there is time

1. **Venue and timing.** FAccT or AIES? The deadline sets the page limit, which decides what
   gets cut from 16 research documents.
2. **The missing baseline.** Mittelstadt et al.'s construction is a per-group floor applied
   by post-processing. I have never run it directly — the closest arm uses group-wise
   thresholds, which is not the same thing. Worth an hour. Should I?
3. **Authorship and AI assistance.** I used an AI assistant substantially for
   implementation, analysis and drafting. What does the department expect me to disclose,
   and how?

## What is not in question

The empirical work is verified. Every headline number is re-derived from stored results by
an automated check that fails if a document and its data disagree. Three of my own
pre-registered predictions failed and are reported as failures. Two of my own claims were
retracted in place. I would rather be told the framing is wrong now than discover it in
review — which is exactly what happened with the remedy, and why it is no longer claimed as
novel.
